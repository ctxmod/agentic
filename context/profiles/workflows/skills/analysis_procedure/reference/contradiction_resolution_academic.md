# Contradiction Resolution

Contradictions between sources signal either methodological differences, scope variations, or genuine disagreement. Resolution requires systematic investigation rather than arbitrary selection.

## Detection

Contradictions emerge during pattern aggregation when:

- Two sources make incompatible claims about the same phenomenon
- Results differ significantly despite similar methodologies
- One source refutes another explicitly
- Statistical findings point in opposite directions

## Resolution Framework

### Step 1: Verify the Contradiction

Confirm the contradiction is genuine rather than apparent:

- **Scope difference**: Do sources study the same population, context, or time period?
- **Definition difference**: Do sources define key terms identically?
- **Measurement difference**: Do sources measure the same construct?
- **Granularity difference**: Does one source report aggregated data that hides the pattern the other detects?

Many apparent contradictions dissolve when scope, definitions, or methods are compared precisely.

### Step 2: Assess Source Quality

When a genuine contradiction exists, evaluate relative credibility:

- **Sample size and power**: Larger, adequately powered studies carry more weight
- **Methodology rigor**: Better-controlled studies are more credible
- **Replication**: Findings replicated across studies outweigh single-study results
- **Recency**: More recent evidence may incorporate improvements
- **Independence**: Studies from independent groups reduce systemic bias

### Step 3: Document Resolution

Record the contradiction and its resolution:

| Aspect | Content |
|--------|---------|
| **Sources** | Which sources conflict |
| **Nature** | What specifically contradicts |
| **Explanation** | Why the contradiction exists (scope, method, genuine) |
| **Resolution** | Which position is better supported and why |
| **Confidence** | How confident is the resolution |
| **Remaining uncertainty** | What would further clarify |

### Step 4: Integrate

Incorporate resolved contradictions into the analysis:

- **Resolved**: Present the stronger position with explanation of why the weaker position exists
- **Unresolved**: Present both positions, explain why resolution is not yet possible, and note what evidence would resolve it
- **Productive**: Some contradictions reveal boundary conditions or moderating factors that enrich understanding

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Consequence |
|--------------|-------------|
| Ignoring contradictions | False confidence in one-sided evidence |
| Cherry-picking | Selecting evidence that supports a preferred conclusion |
| False balance | Treating unequal evidence as equally credible |
| Premature resolution | Declaring resolution without sufficient investigation |
