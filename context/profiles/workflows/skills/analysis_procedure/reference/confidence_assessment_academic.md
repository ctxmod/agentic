# Confidence Assessment

Every claim carries a confidence level reflecting evidence quality, source reliability, and replication status. Documenting confidence enables proportional claims and transparent reasoning.

## Confidence Levels

| Level | Criteria | Language Pattern |
|-------|----------|-----------------|
| **High** | Multiple high-quality sources, consistent findings, peer-reviewed, replicated | "X demonstrates Y" / "The evidence shows Z" |
| **Medium** | Single high-quality source or multiple moderate sources, some inconsistency | "X suggests Y" / "The evidence indicates Z" |
| **Low** | Limited or low-quality sources, contradictory findings, not replicated | "X may be associated with Y" / "Preliminary evidence points to Z" |

## Assessment Dimensions

Evaluate confidence across four dimensions:

### Evidence Quality

- **Strong**: Rigorous methodology, adequate sample, peer-reviewed
- **Moderate**: Reasonable methodology, limited sample, published
- **Weak**: Methodological concerns, small sample, not peer-reviewed

### Consistency

- **Consistent**: Multiple independent sources converge
- **Partially consistent**: Most sources agree, some exceptions
- **Inconsistent**: Sources disagree or contradict

### Replication

- **Replicated**: Findings reproduced by independent researchers
- **Partially replicated**: Some aspects confirmed, others untested
- **Not replicated**: No independent confirmation

### Currency

- **Current**: Recent evidence in an active field
- **Aging**: Older evidence that may be superseded
- **Outdated**: Evidence contradicted or replaced by newer work

## Composite Assessment

Combine dimensions to determine overall confidence:

- All dimensions strong: High confidence
- Most dimensions strong, one weak: Medium-high confidence
- Mixed dimensions: Medium confidence
- Most dimensions weak: Low confidence
- All dimensions weak: Very low confidence (consider whether the claim should be included)

## Documentation Practice

Record confidence alongside every significant claim:

- **Finding**: The specific claim or result
- **Confidence**: Overall level (high/medium/low)
- **Evidence basis**: Sources supporting the claim
- **Caveats**: Conditions limiting confidence
- **Contradictions**: Sources or evidence that reduce confidence

## Confidence-Claim Alignment

Misalignment between confidence and language undermines credibility in both directions: under-hedged low-confidence claims overstate; over-hedged high-confidence claims understate. Match language strength to evidence quality throughout.
