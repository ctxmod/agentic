# Source Evaluation

Structured evaluation frameworks prevent uncritical acceptance of sources. Apply assessment criteria before relying on any source for evidence.

## CRAAP Framework

Evaluate sources across five dimensions:

| Dimension | Assessment Questions |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Currency** | When published? Updated recently? Current enough for the topic? |
| **Relevance** | Addresses the research question? At appropriate depth? |
| **Authority** | Author credentials? Publisher reputation? Institutional affiliation? |
| **Accuracy** | Evidence supported? Peer-reviewed? Verifiable claims? |
| **Purpose** | Inform, teach, persuade, or sell? Evident bias? Conflicts of interest? |

Score each dimension 1-5. Total: 20-25 = high quality, 15-19 = moderate quality, below 15 = low quality.

## SIFT Method

Lateral reading technique for rapid source verification:

1. **Stop**: Before reading deeply, assess source credibility. Check domain, funding, and affiliations.
2. **Investigate**: Research the source's expertise. What do others say about this source?
3. **Find**: Locate the original source of claims. Trace data to primary studies.
4. **Trace**: Verify that citations support the claims made. Check for misinterpretation.

SIFT provides quick pre-screening before investing time in detailed review.

## GRADE Evidence Framework

Assess evidence quality on four levels for claims requiring formal evaluation:

| Level | Description | Characteristics |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| High | True effect close to estimate | Multiple consistent studies, low bias risk |
| Moderate | Moderately confident in estimate | Some inconsistency or indirect evidence |
| Low | Limited confidence in estimate | Significant bias risk or imprecision |
| Very Low | Any estimate very uncertain | Severe limitations across multiple domains |

**Downgrading factors**: risk of bias, inconsistency across studies, indirectness (population or outcome mismatch), imprecision (wide confidence intervals), publication bias.

## Pre-Investment Screening

Before investing time in detailed review, verify:

1. **Publication venue**: Peer-reviewed? Reputable? Relevant tier?
2. **Author credentials**: Domain expertise? Institutional affiliation? Track record?
3. **Citation impact**: How often cited? By whom? Self-citation rate?
4. **Recency**: Current for the field? Superseded by newer work?

## Ongoing Validation

Document validation status for each source:

- **Validated**: Claims verified against evidence; consistent with other sources
- **Contested**: Contradicted by other credible sources; note disagreement
- **Needs verification**: Insufficient evidence to confirm or deny
